ESMFold against AlphaFold2: is the viral deficit an alignment effect?
ESMFold v1 via the ESM Atlas API. All comparisons use only the proteins
both programs scored, so each is judged on the same set.

  viral     106 of 114 scored by ESMFold
  cellular  105 of 119 scored by ESMFold
    skipped, over 400 residues: 22

1. The viral gap under each program (Mann-Whitney U, median TM-score)
  AlphaFold2             viral 0.917  cellular 0.968  p = 1.58e-08  r = -0.450
  ESMFold                viral 0.824  cellular 0.954  p = 1.47e-11  r = -0.538

2. AlphaFold2 against ESMFold on the same proteins (Wilcoxon signed-rank)
  viral     AF2 median 0.917, ESM median 0.824, median AF2 advantage +0.033, p = 4.66e-08
  cellular  AF2 median 0.968, ESM median 0.954, median AF2 advantage +0.006, p = 7.32e-05

3. THE KEY TEST: does AlphaFold2's advantage differ between groups?
   Per protein, advantage = AF2 TM-score minus ESMFold TM-score.
  median advantage: viral +0.033, cellular +0.006
  Mann-Whitney on the advantage: p = 0.0014, r = +0.255
  viral gap in median TM: AF2 0.051, ESMFold 0.130

4. Regression, ESMFold TM-score ~ disorder + is_viral
  n = 211
  ESMFold    is_viral coef -0.2260, p = 1.78e-13;  disorder coef -0.2378, p = 0.00118
  AlphaFold2 is_viral coef -0.0981, p = 2.64e-06;  disorder coef -0.2315, p = 1.02e-05
  (same proteins and model for both, so the coefficients compare directly)

5. Does ESMFold's own confidence track its error?
  Spearman, ESMFold pLDDT vs TM-score: rho = +0.811, p = 1.84e-50 (n = 211)

Reading
  AlphaFold2 gains more over ESMFold on viral proteins than on cellular
  ones. Alignments partly rescue AlphaFold2 on viral proteins, and the
  language model is weaker on them still.
  This is a test of the alignment explanation, not a proof of mechanism:
  ESMFold's language model was itself trained on sequence databases in
  which viral proteins are under-represented.
